org: "Legal aid, multi-office"
challenge: "A multi-office legal aid organization with 120+ staff had fragmented intake processes across three channels (online, phone, clinic). Managing attorneys struggled with inconsistent case acceptance data, unclear transfer workflows, and high follow-up burden. Staff couldn't identify whether intake gaps or capacity constraints were driving rejection patterns. Case acceptance decisions relied on professional judgment and informal capacity conversations rather than shared data on which case types took longest or how caseload varied by office. Underneath it all, the organization had reliable data in some LegalServer fields but discretionary fields were inconsistently used across offices — staff relied on a single paralegal to analyze case data due to lack of capacity and know-how, and didn't trust the reports that did exist."
solution:
  - "Conducted foundational data quality assessment across key LegalServer fields (intake, case acceptance, outcomes, time tracking)"
  - "Documented which fields were complete, which were optional but inconsistently used, and which were missing definitions"
  - "Conducted 30+ structured interviews with intake specialists, attorneys, and supervisors across multiple offices"
  - "Mapped current intake workflows from first contact through case acceptance, including transfer and escalation points"
  - "Analyzed LegalServer data and call system metrics to identify bottlenecks and decision-point delays"
  - "Analyzed case acceptance data to identify patterns: which case types were most frequently rejected, which took longest to resolve, which generated the most follow-up"
  - "Computed time-to-disposition by case type and office to reveal true capacity bottlenecks"
  - "Assessed historical impact of prior case acceptance policy changes to evaluate effectiveness"
  - "Examined internal referral patterns to identify informal specialization and quantify centralization opportunities"
  - "Created simple, stand-alone analyses (intake completion rates, time from contact to disposition, rejection patterns by case type) that built to tell a narrative of organizational pain points"
  - "Documented pain points and developed recommendations for triage refinement and staffing alignment"
results:
  - text: "Identified that intake data quality was adequate for analysis"
  - text: "Built staff confidence in data through transparent methodology and clear limitations"
  - text: "Identified high-impact workflow improvements with measurable impact on staff time"
  - text: "Clarified intake decision criteria that reduced downstream follow-up work"
  - text: "Quantified impact of prior case acceptance policy changes with real data"
  - text: "Identified specific caller segments that took significant staff time away from intake"
  - text: "Provided evidence for conversations about centralization vs. regional capacity"
  - text: "Enabled informed staffing and budget decisions"
technologies: "LegalServer data audit, SQL queries, data completeness analysis, documentation frameworks, Vonage call data, structured interviews, workflow mapping diagrams, time tracking analysis, comparative case type analysis, historical policy impact assessment"
